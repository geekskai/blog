# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are English-language search visitors who arrive with a specific task and expect a useful result without learning a complex product.

Secondary users include registered creators, DJs, and music-library organizers who repeatedly prepare audio they own and need private, browser-local batch workflows. Developers and technical readers are audience segments for specific tools and editorial content, not the default audience for every Geekskai surface.

## Product Purpose

Geekskai is a portfolio of focused browser tools that help people complete concrete tasks with minimal setup. The public tool portfolio is the acquisition layer; Geekskai Audio Toolkit is its flagship authenticated workspace.

Audio Toolkit lets users normalize, convert, and batch-prepare audio they own while keeping file processing on their device.

## Positioning

Geekskai invests in tools when observed demand, attainable competition, policy-safe value, maintenance cost, and evidence of a successful result justify the work. Tool count alone is not a product goal.

When a promised outcome can be delivered reliably in the browser, local processing is the default. Ordinary acquisition tools keep their core result open without forced registration. Account features may add quota, continuity, or batch convenience without redefining the open-tool promise.

## Operating Context

Most visitors enter through search on a single-purpose tool, complete the immediate task, and may then continue to one to three relevant tools in the same workflow.

English-language organic demand is the acquisition priority. Existing localized experiences remain available, and further localization investment follows their own search and product evidence rather than an equal-investment rule.

Audio Toolkit is a distinct signed-in workspace for local audio preparation. Its Audio Credits and batch capabilities are separate from public downloader allowances and ordinary open-tool access.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Public tools, public downloader allowances, Audio Credits, and paid capabilities are separate product contracts and must not be presented as interchangeable.
- Analytics may record lifecycle events and tool identity, but must not record a user's tool inputs, result payloads, audio, filenames, track lists, or local workspace data.
- Audio Toolkit processes audio files on the user's device. Server-side systems manage only the identity, Credit reservation, settlement, and billing state required to authorize the workflow.
- Audio Toolkit projects, presets, and recent activity are device-bound and are not promised as cloud-synchronized data.
- Commercialization remains subject to an explicit payment launch gate. Passing code checks, configuring credentials, or deploying code does not by itself open paid access.
- Product and interface claims must stay consistent with current legal, pricing, entitlement, and release controls.

## Brand Commitments

- Preserve the Geekskai name, the existing logo assets, the Geeks Kai author identity, and `support@geekskai.com` as the support contact.
- Use a direct, technically credible, outcome-oriented voice. Explain what a tool does, what remains local, and what restrictions apply without inflated language.
- Do not invent customers, testimonials, performance results, revenue, market leadership, payment availability, or social proof.
- Treat visual styling as a separate design-system concern; colors, typography, layout recipes, and component rules do not belong in this product record.

## Evidence on Hand

- The current repository exposes 51 tool entries and ten locale configurations, alongside technical articles, an author page, legal pages, pricing surfaces, and the implemented Audio Toolkit workflow.
- Repository code and product documentation support the open-tool, local-first, quota, Audio Credit, and commercialization-gate boundaries recorded above.
- The root README retains template-era and point-in-time performance claims. Those claims are not approved product evidence unless they are independently reverified against the current production site.
- No verified customer testimonials, customer case studies, or externally validated performance benchmarks were found for use as product proof. Future design work must not fabricate them.

## Product Principles

1. **Outcome first.** Make the promised result and the path to it immediately understandable.
2. **Open core access.** Let ordinary tool visitors complete the core task without forced registration.
3. **Local-first privacy.** Keep inputs and results on the device whenever the outcome can be delivered reliably there.
4. **Demand-led investment.** Prioritize tools and clusters using observed demand and successful-outcome evidence.
5. **Evidence-only claims.** Publish only product, trust, pricing, and performance claims that current evidence supports.
